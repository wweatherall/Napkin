A worker session does at most 3 jobs in a row: after job.py done it takes the next Ready job; when none is Ready it waits for one in the background (job.py take --wait, up to WORKER_WAIT, 20 min) and is woken when it gets one; after its 3rd job, or a wait that times out, it stops and tells you it's ready for a /clear. job.py counts jobs per session name and refuses a 4th.

Why: Your call, 2026-11-22: keeps a worker's context small without you clearing sessions after every job; 3 in total to start, see how it works; 20 min wait is one check-in interval.
