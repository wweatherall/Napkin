# P44 — A brief's rule says what it's for as well as what to do.

A brief's rule says what it's for as well as what to do. A worker may depart from a rule when following it to the letter would defeat its purpose or break something the code relies on: it does what serves the purpose and adds a 'Changed from the brief:' line to its response (what the brief said, what it did, why); the integrator reviews every one. Not for anything William would notice (behaviour, visible wording, what is saved: ask him), and never to drop a 'What can fail' case or a check, only to change how one is met.

Why: William, 2026-11-22: H102's brief had a rule that left RELEASE_DEG below INFER_DEG (an axis would let go before it sticks); the worker built it as written and tested it. Exact briefs carry the integrator's mistakes exactly; a stated purpose lets the worker catch them.
